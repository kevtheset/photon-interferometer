01 / Split the amplitude

At BS1, the front coated face reflects and transmits equal amplitudes. Both arms belong to the same photon.

At BS2, the back coated face combines the two amplitudes. Their relative phase sets the output probabilities.

Both output amplitudes travel to the detectors. At detection, the state localizes to one spot and exactly one counter increases.

Surface geometry & physics model

The gold line is the splitting coating; the parallel blue line is the other glass face. BS1’s coating is on the front face encountered by the laser. BS2’s coating is on the back face encountered by the upper-arm beam, which traverses the glass first. The lower-arm beam reaches that same coating from the opposite side. All reflection/transmission occurs at the gold line. The blue side edges close each glass plate. At each air–glass boundary the path bends according to Snell’s law with refractive index 1.5, returning to its original direction after a parallel plate. Plate thickness and lateral offsets are exaggerated for visibility. The wavepackets follow these paths and travel more slowly inside the glass. Both arms have equal optical lengths; detector positions give equal output transit times. The top mirror’s backing is behind its near reflecting surface.

Optional path measurement. This is an ideal projective, nondestructive measurement, with unit efficiency and no false clicks. When a packet’s center reaches the upper-arm sensing position, a click (probability ½) keeps the entire upper packet and removes the lower one; a no-click result (probability ½) keeps the entire lower packet and removes the upper one. The surviving packet immediately has amplitude 1. A no-click result is informative because the ideal measurement is made at the known arrival time. This instantaneous whole-packet projection is an educational simplification, not a time-resolved model of a real detector. The photon is not absorbed. At BS2, the surviving arm produces two packets of amplitude 1/√2, giving equal output probabilities for every phase. The two outcomes are classical alternatives, not coherent amplitudes to be added. Both packet colors remain the same.

With the which-path detector off, for the output labels and phase reference used here, P(PM1) = cos²(Δφ/2) and P(PM2) = sin²(Δφ/2). Packet height scales with the square root of the probability carried by that packet: the incoming packet has relative height 1, each arm has height 1/√2 ≈ 0.71, and the outputs have heights √P(PM1) and √P(PM2). The faint envelope makes these size differences visible. All wavepackets use the same color. The drawn oscillations represent complex probability amplitudes, not a measured electric field or two half-photons. The envelope and carrier are schematic, and propagation is greatly slowed. Output amplitudes are recombined coherently, then one outcome is sampled using the Born rule at detector arrival.

“Laser” represents an idealized stream of independent single-photon packets, with Poisson-distributed emission times. An actual attenuated laser is not a deterministic single-photon source. Detectors here have perfect efficiency, no dead time and no dark counts. Tallies last until reset, phase change, detector toggle or page reload. Motion speed scales simulated time, including emission rate.
